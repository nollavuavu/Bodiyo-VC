## Problems (fix only)

1. **Topic 5, (a) "Easy sets and warm-ups don't count" / (c) Robinson 2024 "supports only counting sets taken near failure"** ([10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-024-02069-2), PMID 38970765). **Fix.** Robinson shows a continuous dose-response: growth rises as estimated RIR falls. It doesn't show that sets far from failure contribute nothing. Suggested text: "Sets stopped far from failure stimulate less growth, so Carlsen only counts sets within about 0–3 RIR as hard sets (a counting convention, not a proven cutoff)." Change the citation note to "supports more growth closer to failure."
2. **Topic 5, (a) "Spreading hard sets across sessions gets more out of each one"** (Pelland 2026, [10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w), PMID 41343037). **Fix.** None of the cited papers shows this for hypertrophy. Pelland found frequency effects on hypertrophy "compatible with negligible effects" (the benefit was for strength), and Schoenfeld 2019 found no effect once volume is equated. Suggested text: "Spreading sets across sessions keeps each session shorter and fresher; growth is similar either way when weekly hard sets are equal." Present it as practical reasoning, not a finding.
3. **Topic 8, (a) "Different exercises for the same muscle can grow different regions"**, cited to Zabaleta-Korta 2023 ([10.5114/jhk/163561](https://doi.org/10.5114/jhk/163561), PMID 37559762). **Fix.** This study found **no** group or group×region differences (p = 0.367). Only the preacher-curl group grew, and only at the distal 70% site, with no growth in the incline-curl group. That is weak, within-group evidence. Suggested change: base this sentence on Kassiano 2022 (PMID 35438660: "some degree of systematic variation seems to enhance regional hypertrophic adaptations"), and describe Zabaleta-Korta as "one small study hinting the preacher curl grows the lower biceps; no significant difference between exercises."
4. **Topic 8, (b) "choose 1–2 stable exercises that are hardest near the stretched position"**, with Larsen 2025 ([10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2025.1611468), PMID 40692697) and Schwanbeck 2020 (PMID 32358310). **Fix.** The recommendation is phrased as a firm rule, but Larsen found dumbbell and cable lateral raises equal despite different resistance profiles (moderate to extreme Bayes support for no difference). Schwanbeck found free weights and machines equal, so stability had no detectable effect. Evidence.md's own (d) admits this, but the (a)/(b) app text doesn't. Suggested text: "When in doubt, prefer exercises that challenge the stretched position and let you push close to failure safely; head-to-head trials often show similar growth, so pick what you can progress consistently."
5. **Topic 7, (b) "add lengthened partials at the end of a set once full reps fail"**, and (a) "partial reps in the stretched half ... can match or beat full reps" (Wolf 2023 IJSC [10.47206/ijsc.v3i1.182](https://doi.org/10.47206/ijsc.v3i1.182); Pedrosa 2022 PMID 33977835; Kassiano 2023 PMID 37015016). **Fix.** None of the cited trials tested partials added *after* full-ROM failure. They compared partial-only training with full-ROM training. In Wolf 2023 the main model slightly favoured **full** ROM (SMD 0.12), and the long-length partial subgroup estimate (−0.28) had a CI crossing zero (−0.81 to 0.16). Suggested text for (a): "Partial reps in the stretched part of the range grew muscle about as well as, and in a few small studies better than, full reps." Label (b) as "an untested extension of this research; optional."
